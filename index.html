A green dashboard is not a release decision.

The team that builds an agent usually tests it too, with generic scores like accuracy and helpfulness. Those numbers look fine while the agent does something your compliance team would never sign off.

We ran a full validation on a popular open-source airline agent. It routed requests well and passed its guardrail checks. It also rebooked a customer onto a new flight without asking, and showed a stranger's booking in 2 of the 4 privacy tests.

Only rules written for that business would catch either one.

Questions teams ask first.

Is this a certification?

No. A certification tests against a fixed standard. We validate your system against criteria your own owners sign, and give a verdict with evidence. The report is designed to support your audits, your procurement reviews and your own release decision.

Does our data leave our environment?

No. The runner, the tracing and the results live in your cloud account. We work through your access, and you can switch it off at any time.

Do you use an AI model to grade the answers?

Only when no written rule can decide, and we tell you when we do. By default every score comes from a rule you can read, applied to a recorded conversation, so any verdict can be reproduced.

Will you fix the problems you find?

We trace each failure to its likely cause in code and recommend the fix. Your team, or a partner, makes the change. Keeping the builder and the validator separate is what keeps the verdict independent.

What do we need to provide?

An endpoint for the agent system in a test or staging environment, a few anonymised examples of real traffic, and an hour each week from the people who own product, compliance and operations.

We already use Braintrust or Langfuse. Why add you?

Keep them. They are where your team builds and debugs. We add the independent part: criteria signed before testing, a golden set your experts approve, and a verdict from someone who isn't grading their own work.
